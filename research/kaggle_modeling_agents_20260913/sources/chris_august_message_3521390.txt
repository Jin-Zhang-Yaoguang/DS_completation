As long as a human is needed to prompt the input and to eval the outputs, ML is still going to have a human in the loop.
I've heard to many manager talking about LLMs used for stuff they certainly are not the right fit, because how should they know, agent confirm their biases and run with it.
Even GPT Astra, the first model which really makes me rethink the future preds is doing it.
Labs for obvs reason align the models (with the exception of grok maybe), but humans are not "aligned", their guardrailes are rules defined by the society they live in, those rules can be harmful or beneficial, the humans can break them or question them. We see such behaviour during training in LLMs, but the final result will converge to a certain predefined ruleset. This lack of variaety can also be seen in LLM writing, which lacks variability.
Maybe in the future LLMs have more flavors, but i doubt huge variety , because its risky and potentially harmful. Look at people like Sir Ronald Aylmer Fisher, one of the greatest researchers of the 20th century, but a fanatic believer in eugenics. Maybe for real groundbreaking research across different domainspeople with vastly diverging mindsets from the average are needed. Who knows.
